Where they agree
The verse affirms God's absolute dominion and self-sufficiency, and the Kursī is real and immense. Whatever it is, it implies no need, no fatigue and no limitation in Him — the closing words of the verse are read as settling exactly that.
Where they differ
3 positions · shown by weight of support
What is the Kursī?
A created thing, immense, distinct from the ʿArsh and smaller than it
majority
Read as its plain sense. Supported by the report likening the Kursī beside the ʿArsh to a ring thrown into an open desert, and by the ʿArsh being named separately elsewhere in the Qur'an 7:54.
Ibn Kathīr · al-Ṭabarī · al-Qurṭubī · al-Baghawī — Ibn Kathīr, Tafsīr, 2:255 §4 · al-Ṭabarī, Jāmiʿ al-Bayān, 5/398
His knowledge — the word standing for what His knowing encompasses
reported
Taken as figurative, on the pattern of naming a thing by what it holds. Read alongside the verse's own statement that people grasp nothing of His knowledge except what He wills 20:110.
Reported from Ibn ʿAbbās — recorded by al-Ṭabarī, Jāmiʿ al-Bayān, 5/397; noted by Ibn Kathīr
The ʿArsh itself — the two words naming one thing
minority
Rejected by most, who hold that the sources distinguish the two and that the ring-in-the-desert report rules the identification out.
al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī — al-Qurṭubī, al-Jāmiʿ, 3/277, with the rebuttal
