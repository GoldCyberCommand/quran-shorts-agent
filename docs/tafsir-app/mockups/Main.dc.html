اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ ۚ لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ ۚ لَّهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ ۗ مَن ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِندَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ ۚ يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ ۖ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيْءٍ مِّنْ عِلْمِهِ إِلَّا بِمَا شَاءَ ۚ وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ ۖ وَلَا يَئُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَا ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَلِيُّ الْعَظِيمُ
Allah — there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of existence. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission? He knows what is before them and what will be after them, and they encompass not a thing of His knowledge except for what He wills. His Kursī extends over the heavens and the earth, and their preservation tires Him not. And He is the Most High, the Most Great.
Ṣaḥīḥ International
In plain English
This is the Qur'an's fullest single statement about God. He alone is worthy of worship. He never tires, never sleeps, and never stops holding up what He made. Everything in the heavens and the earth is His, and nobody can speak up on anyone else's behalf without His permission. He knows what lies ahead of people and behind them, while they grasp nothing of His knowledge beyond what He allows them20:110

Context where it sits · setting of revelation
No specific occasion of revelation is established for this verse. It sits in a long stretch of Al-Baqarah on faith and rejection — directly after the call to spend before the Day comes when no bargaining helps (2:254), and directly before "there is no compulsion in religion" (2:256). Several sound reports record the Prophet ﷺ singling it out as the greatest verse in the Qur'an.

What scholars said 1 agreement · 3 positions on Kursī
Where they agree
The verse affirms God's absolute dominion and self-sufficiency, and the Kursī is real and immense. Whatever it is, it implies no need or limitation in Him.
Where they differ — what is the Kursī?
A created thing, immense, distinct from and smaller than the ʿArsh.
Majority · Ibn Kathīr, al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Baghawī
His knowledge — the word standing for what His knowing encompasses.
Reported from Ibn ʿAbbās · recorded by al-Ṭabarī
The ʿArsh itself, the two words naming one thing.
al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī · a minority reading, rejected by most
